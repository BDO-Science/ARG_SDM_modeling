American River Fall-Run Chinook — Modeling Notes

Overview

This document summarizes the modeling framework used in the Shiny application and precompute script. The model simulates the salmon life cycle from spawners to age-structured returns under alternative flow and temperature scenarios.

Key components:

  • Spawn timing: Ordinal cumulative logit model (CLMM) with October/November mean temperatures and a year random effect (forecasted via AR(1)).
  • Egg → fry survival: Temperature-dependent mortality (TDM), exponential or linear-threshold, across incubation windows defined by ATUs.
  • Pre-spawn adult survival: Logistic function of cumulative pre-spawn degree-days.
  • Density dependence & rear survival: Fry production capped via Beverton–Holt term; constant fry→smolt survival.
  • Smolt-to-adult returns (SAR): Deterministic or stochastic SAR with ages 3–5.
  • Calibration: Joint optimization of mean SAR and rear survival against observed escapement (2011–2024).
  • Forecasting: Projected spawner abundance trajectories under scenarios.

Master Life-Cycle Recursion

Spawner abundance in future years is updated recursively:

\[ S_{y+a} \;\leftarrow\; S_{y+a} + S_y \cdot f \cdot S_{\mathrm{pre},y} \cdot F \cdot S_{\mathrm{egg},y} \cdot dd_y \cdot S_{\mathrm{rear}} \cdot \mathrm{SAR}_y \cdot P(\mathrm{age}=a), \quad a \in \{3,4,5\}. \]

This recursion integrates all biological processes: pre-spawn mortality, incubation survival, density dependence, rearing survival, SAR, and age structure.

Process Modules

1. Spawn Timing

Spawning is distributed across 11 fixed 10-day bins from October–January.

  • Model: ordinal cumulative logit mixed model (CLMM):

    \[ \Pr(B \le k \mid y) = \operatorname{logit}^{-1}(\zeta_k - \eta_y), \]

    \[ \eta_y = \beta_{\text{Oct}} \overline{T}^{\*}_{y,\text{Oct}} + \beta_{\text{Nov}} \overline{T}^{\*}_{y,\text{Nov}} + u_y, \]

    with thresholds (_k), fixed temperature slopes, and year random effect (u_y).

  • Forecasting: (u_y) evolves via AR(1):
    (u_y = u_{y-1} + _y, ;_y (0,^2)).

  • Simulation: For each redd, a bin is drawn from probabilities (p_{y,b}), then a uniform date is sampled within the bin’s calendar window.

Outcome: year-to-year variability in spawn dates reflecting temperature covariates and persistent random effects.
No flow covariates are included.

2. Pre-Spawn Survival

Adults accumulate thermal stress prior to spawning. Survival is logistic in cumulative degree-days:

\[ S_{\mathrm{pre},y} = \operatorname{logit}^{-1}(\theta_0 + \theta_1 \cdot \mathrm{DD}_y), \]

\[ \mathrm{DD}_y = \sum_{t=\text{Oct 1}}^{\text{spawn}_y} \max(T_t - T_{\text{base}}, 0). \]

  • Low degree-days → high survival.
  • High degree-days → survival declines steeply.

3. Egg → Fry Survival (TDM)

Incubation window length is set by accumulated thermal units (ATUs):

\[ D_{\text{egg}} = \frac{958}{T}, \qquad D_{\text{fry}} = \frac{417}{T}. \]

Survival is computed as:

\[ S_{\mathrm{egg},y} = \exp(-H_y), \]

where (H_y) is the cumulative hazard across incubation days.

  • Exponential TDM (WaterForum 2020, SALMOD 2006):

    \[ H_y = \sum_i \alpha \, e^{\beta T_i}. \]

  • Linear-threshold TDM (Martin et al. 2017):

    \[ H_y = \alpha \sum_i \max(T_i - \beta, 0). \]

Exponentiation converts additive daily hazards into multiplicative survival, capturing how even small daily risks accumulate into nonlinear survival losses.

4. Density Dependence & Rear Survival

Eggs surviving incubation and degree-day effects face fry density dependence:

\[ \text{redds}_y = S_y \cdot f \cdot S_{\mathrm{pre},y}, \qquad \text{eggs}_y = \text{redds}_y \cdot F, \]

\[ dd_y = \frac{S_0}{1 + \text{redds}_y / K_y}, \qquad \text{fry\_dd}_y = \text{eggs}_y \cdot S_{\mathrm{egg},y} \cdot dd_y, \]

\[ \text{reared}_y = \text{fry\_dd}_y \cdot S_{\mathrm{rear}}, \qquad S_{\mathrm{eff},y} = S_{\mathrm{egg},y} \cdot dd_y. \]

5. Smolt → Adult Returns (SAR)

Reared smolts return as adults after age-structured delays:

\[ S_{y+a} \leftarrow S_{y+a} + \text{reared}_y \cdot \mathrm{SAR}_y \cdot P(\mathrm{age}=a), \quad a \in \{3,4,5\}. \]

  • Deterministic SAR: constant (y = {}).
  • Stochastic SAR: drawn from Normal, Lognormal, Beta, or Gamma distributions, with optional timing filters:
    • all years stochastic,
    • block years stochastic,
    • or pulse years with shocks.

Calibration & Forecasting

Calibration (2011–2024)

  • Targets: observed escapement.

  • Seeds: first 3 years fixed from observations.

  • Fit window: years 4–14.

  • Objective: minimize SSE between observed and predicted spawners:

    \[ \min_{\mathrm{SAR}_{\text{mean}}, S_{\mathrm{rear}}} \sum_{y \in \text{fit}} \big(\widehat{S}_y - S^{\mathrm{obs}}_y\big)^2. \]

  • Method: optim(..., method="L-BFGS-B").

Forecasting

  1. Spawn dates simulated from CLMM + AR(1).
  2. Degree-days computed per brood year.
  3. Egg→fry survival from TDM.
  4. Fry capped by density dependence.
  5. SAR applied deterministically or stochastically.

Forecasts yield modeled spawner abundance trajectories by environment and TDM variant.
