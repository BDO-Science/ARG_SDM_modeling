## Panelist 1

*Weights: Martin 0.25, Bratovich 0.25, Bartholow 0.50*

Martin model for winter-run chinook and assumes equal sensitivity across entire development period. In addition, I would expect salmon eggs to have a non-linear temperature dependent survival relationship to temperature. Water forum model predicts alevin stage to be more sensitive to temperature, however, overall predicts very low temperature sensitivity. Salmod model data unclear, but egg and alevin stage sensitivies appear most realistic and exponential temperature dependent survival relationship seems biologically plausible to me.

## Panelist 2

*Weights: Martin 0.10, Bratovich 0.90, Bartholow 0.00*

While the mechanism for TDM  identified in Martin et al (2017) is applicable to other populations of salmon, the model has not been validated for any fall-run CS nor in the temperature ranges expected in the LAR during fall-run spawning and egg incubation. The range of river temperatures considered during fit of the Martin et al (2017) model were all < 57 F (14C), and the water temperatures being evaluated in the Power Bypass Alternatives are almost exclusively higher than that range . Additionally, LAR fall-run have evolved under different water temperatures than winter-run and may have different temperature tolerance. Because of these factors, I think it is likely that Martin et al overestimates LAR fall-run TDM at water temperatures <~57 F and underestimates TDM at water temperatures >~57 F.  TDM calculations generated by the Martin model for LAR fall-run would be most useful when interpreted relative to each other and not considered actual estimates of mortality. Because the model that has been developed for the SDM process attempts to approximate the "true" value of TDM as a part of a larger life-cycle simulation, this is problematic (until/unless it is proven to accurately predict TDM for LAR fall-run). Therefore, I rated the Martin model lower for its specific application in the SDM process.  (Score = 0.10)

In comparison, the Bratovich et al tool (2020) was developed specifically for the purposes of looking at the survival effects of a Power Bypass on Chinook Salmon in American River, and development intentionally considered the temperature regime and population of salmon  spawning in the LAR. Because it relies largely on laboratory studies, however, it could underestimate TDM in the natural environment. The tool partially accounts for this by adjusting survival lower from hatch through the alevin stage; this adjustment is also consistent with the physiological mechanisms identified in Martin et al. (2017), Anderson (2021), and others, as oxygen demand increases from eggs to alevin. A weighted average of the Bratovich tool and the Martin model would reduce that bias for water temperatures <57 F.  (Score = 0.9)

Bartholow and Heasley (2006) is over two decades old and has not been updated to reflect recent advances in science that have updated our understanding of the effects of water temperature on early life stage survival of Chinook Salmon (e.g., Martin et al. 2017; Martin et al. 2020; Anderson et al. 2018). Additionally, SALMOD no longer appears to be supported by the USGS, as CDFW’s attempts to contact the developers were unanswered (CDFW's Sites Reservoir Operations ITP Effects Analysis, 2024). My understanding is that this model has been used to model TDM in the LAR not because it is expected to provide the best estimate, but because it once represented best available science and there was a desire to compare TDM predictions to prior years when the model was more widely applied. I don't see much value in using this model in the SDM process unless others are particularly attached to it. (Score = 0.0)

## Panelist 3

*Weights: Martin 0.20, Bratovich 0.70, Bartholow 0.10*

The Martin model was designed for WRCS on the Sacramento and there are likely to be some differences between these fish and the FRCS in the LAR. It uses a 57 degree temperature, which we almost never see on the LAR until mid/late November.  Bartholow and Heasley (2006)/SALMOD is older and doesn’t incorporate more recent information.

## Panelist 5

*Weights: Martin 0.40, Bratovich 0.30, Bartholow 0.30*

This weighting strategy is based on the understanding that mortality at hatch for salmon eggs is primarily influenced by two factors: an imbalance between oxygen supply and demand at high temperatures and embryonic damage from thermal stress before hatching. Unlike alevin, eggs are immobile, can't use gills for respiration, and have a high metabolic demand relative to their surface area, making them particularly vulnerable to temperature stress. This vulnerability can lead to delayed mortality at hatch, especially if they are exposed to high temperatures during critical developmental stages like gastrulation (Martin et al 2020, Anderson et al 2022, Bloomer et al 2022). 

The Martin et al. (2020) model received the highest weight (45%) because it accurately reflects this heightened sensitivity of the egg stage. However, it's important to note that this model was parameterized using data from Winter-run Chinook salmon, a species that may be adapted to colder waters than the Lower American River.
In contrast, the Bratovich et al. (2020) model was given a lower weight (20%) because it anticipates the alevin stage to be more sensitive to temperature than the egg stage, which contradicts our understanding of the factors affecting egg mortality.
The Bartholow and Heasley (2006) model was weighted at 35% and is included to provide additional evidence in an ensemble with the other two models.
Given the uncertainty in predicting thermal mortality, we gave relatively higher weight to the Martin et al. (2020) model because of its linear, stage-independent approach to mortality. This is a form of hedging, as the other models accrue temperature-dependent mortality at higher temperatures and anticipate effects across a narrower temperature range.
